WHAT THE TEAM DISCUSSED

Six ideas worth carrying forward

These cards summarize direct meeting evidence. They do not claim that the full automation or every example has already been implemented.

01 Direct evidence
KB

Search a shared "What's Worked" library

Point GitHub Copilot CLI at the team's accumulated techniques, memory setups, project patterns, and best practices before starting a new problem.

Why it mattersReuse proven approaches instead of rediscovering them.
02 Direct evidence
BI

Query Kusto and Power BI in natural language

Ask for case distributions, representative counts, P50 or P90 values, and other operational metrics without first knowing the correct KQL, tables, or reports.

Why it mattersShorten the path from a business question to usable evidence.
03 Kos proposal
QL

Create an AI PM "quest list"

Maintain a growing catalog of capabilities every AI PM can learn, such as querying data through CLI, creating AI-assisted videos, and documenting reusable workflows.

Why it mattersMake skill development visible, practical, and cumulative.
04 Kos proposal
5M

Use a five-minute meeting contribution

Let one person demonstrate a useful technique during the regular team meeting, then capture it while the context, examples, and questions are still fresh.

Why it mattersAttach documentation to existing team behavior.
05 Agreed direction
GH

Move reusable work into team GitHub spaces

Store reusable code and project assets in shared team repositories rather than relying on personal repositories and one-off access grants.

Why it mattersImprove continuity, access, and reuse across projects.
06 Demonstrated example
RP

Turn fast prototypes into reusable playbooks

The Room Pulse example showed how quickly an idea can become a working prototype. The next value comes from sharing the complete process, not only the final demo.

Why it mattersMake the method portable, not just the artifact impressive.

THE INFERRED OPERATING MODEL

A self-reinforcing team learning loop

This model was not stated as one formal architecture, but it is strongly supported by the discussion: CLI helps the team contribute knowledge, and that shared knowledge makes CLI more useful for the next person and project.

Important constraint Allison noted that permission changes and extraction reliability can break fully automatic workflows. The loop needs explicit failure handling and source checks.
